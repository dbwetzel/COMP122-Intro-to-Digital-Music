# 3.1 Synthesis Theory

*Algorithms, architecture, and parameters*

![simple synth architecture](images/Simple_synth.png "diagram: oscillator -> envelope -> amp")

## What is a synthesizer?
* A machine that generates sound electronically, often digitally.
* Synthesizers may be stand-alone devices (keyboards, rack-mounted, sound modules, etc.)
* “Virtual Instruments” are synthesizers that exist as software (stand-alone or as a "plugin" to other software)

## How do they work?
Most synthesizers, at their most basic level, share a common structure. There is a sound generating source, usually some kind of oscillator, an amplitude "envelope" controller that defines and articulates an individual note, and a final volume control.

| Component | Function |
| --- | --- |
|Oscillator<br> <img src="images/osc.png" width=90> |  Generates a basic tone or sound: <br> - Sine/Triangle/Sawtooth/Square wave <br> - Noise or other complex wave shape <br> - Sampled sound |
| Envelope Generator<br><img src="images/env.png" width=90>| Controls the attack and release of a note (amplitude over time)<br>Attack (A) – the speed of the attack (~0 to 250 msec) <br>Decay (D) – time after attack to settle to sustain volume <br>Sustain (S) – volume level to hold at while key is pressed <br>Release (R) – Time it takes to fade to silence after key is released | 
| Amplifier<br><img src="images/amp.png" width=70> | Controls the overall volume of the sound going to the speakers |

Beyond this basic architecture, synthesizers can become much more complex, adding filters, LFO controls, parallel algorithms, and innovative strategies for sound generation.

![complex synth architecture](images/complex-synth.png "diagram: multiple oscillators -> filters -> envelope -> amp + LFO")

## Common synthesizer algorithm types

* Additive – combines banks of simple oscillators to generate complex timbres
* Subtractive – filters complex tones to create rich timbres
* Frequency Modulation (FM) – oscillator frequency modulated by a second oscillator to create complex timbres
* Sampler – recorded sound is modified with filters and envelope generators
* Physical modeling – algorithms mimic the operation of physical instruments (buzz + filter)
