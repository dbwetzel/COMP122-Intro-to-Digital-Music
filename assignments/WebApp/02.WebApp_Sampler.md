# Web App #2 - Sample Player
*Prepare a set of short audio samples for use in a non-linear interactive music system*

In this creative project, you will add a set of playable audio samples to an interactive musical web app. Ultimately, this project will combine samples with beats, sequences, and automated generative music. If all goes according to plan, these different elements will work together to make a cohesive musical experience that you can even share with others.

There are four steps to this project: 
1. preparing samples, 
2. Setting up your web app, 
3. Tagging and testing samples, and 
4. Saving your work.

## Step 1 - Prepare some Samples

1. **Locate recorded sounds** that you would like to use for your project. Keep in mind that you can add or replace samples in this project at a later date, so please don't let the "perfect" be the enemy of the "good." The goal is to get something working as soon as possible. You can refine and improve it later.
2. The sounds you choose may be **musical** (a keyboard riff, a breakbeat, a guitar solo, etc) or **"non" musical** (spoken word, environmental sounds, noises, etc.)
3. Your samples should be **Public Domain, your own creation, or used by permission.** If you use copyrighted material, please be advised that you cannot distribute your work publicly, but it may be considered "fair use" within the bounds of this course. Please be sure to document your sources.
4. **Edit your sound** files from the original source in order to trim it to just the part that you want
5. Samples should be relatively short, cleanly edited, and balanced for volume if necessary ("normalized"). A typical sample should be a few bars of music, or a few seconds of sound.
6. Make sure to **save** your sample files as **.wav** or **.mp3**. Mp3 is preferable, since your files need to be less than 5MB.
7. **Organize** your sound files together in a **folder** on your own computer or OneDrive (or other storage location/media of your choice)

### Rules for sampling audio:

- Sounds must be recorded by you OR if taken from another source you must give a full citation in your Sakai submission (give a source citation for your own sound recordings, too)
- Use at least three different sound samples
- Feel free to use whatever tools you like to transform the sound samples before uploading to the WebApp code editor
- You may use individual loops from Soundtrap for this project if you are having trouble finding other suitable samples
- Sound files must be less than 5 MB in size (P5 editor restriction).

## Step 2 - Test and tag your samples

1. Open the Sample Player web app: 
 - <a href = "https://editor.p5js.org/dbwetzel/full/gTlEyB2ei" target="_blank">https://editor.p5js.org/dbwetzel/full/gTlEyB2ei</a>
2. Start the metronome to make sure you have sound and that the player is working (stop the metronome once you have confirmed it works)
3. Load a sample,
 - Add a new sample player by clicking the button that says "Add new sample player"
 - Choose a sound file from your computer by clicking the button that says "Load File" 
 - Give it a name (this is a label for your sample in the player app)
 - Specify any desired pitch transposition and fine tuning.
  - Transposition values are given as semitones (1 semitone is the difference between C and C-sharp).
  - Tuning is given in "cents." 100 cents is equal to a semitone. Transposition and tuning values may be positive or negative.
 - Specify a base tempo (beats per minute)
    - if the tempo you specify is different from the player tempo, your sample will time stretch to match the player tempo.
    - The default is 120 bpm. If you imported a sample with a tempo of 70 bpm, enter "70" in this field to make your sample play in time with the metronome. In this case it will play faster if the metronome is set at 120 bpm.
 - Specify loop duration (in seconds or Tone.js "Notation" or "Transport Time"). 
    - The default is "1m" (one measure).
    - The word "sample" will set the loop to the length of the sample.
    - A plain number value will be interpreted as loop time in seconds.
 - Feel free to modify the "Grain size" and "Overlap" if it makes your sample sound better when time-stretched. Or leave the default values alone. These control the smoothness of granular playback. This is what allows for independent time stretching and pitch shifting.
    - Grain size is the length of the individual audio grain in seconds (default is 0.1 seconds or 100 milliseconds).
    - Overlap is the amount of time to crossfade between grains.
4. For each entry, see your changes formatted as JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) in the "Sampler Data" window at the bottom of the page.
5. You should be able to play your samples directly within the sample player. If the metronome beat is turned on, your samples might play in time (if the BPM field is calibrated correctly). Your samples will play simultaneously if you click the play buttons fast enough. Make sure they play back before proceeding to the next step.

## Step 3 - Save your work

Once you have a set of samples loaded in the sample player (at least 3 samples, please!) with correct and complete tags, you will need to save your work. In the future, all of this will happen on a secure web site where you just save your work in the cloud as you go (like Soundtrap). However, this assignment is part of a prototype project, and we have a couple slightly tricky steps to go through. **Please reach out for help if you get stuck on this part!**

1. **Download** a .zip archive of your sample files (.mp3 or .wav) plus your data file "samples.json" that describes and tags all your samples for the player app.
 - Click the button that says "Download .zip"
 - Check your Downloads folder for a file called "sampler.zip." A .zip file is a "compressed archive" file that allows multiple files and folders to be downloaded as a single file.
 - On a Mac, your .zip file might extract automatically as a folder called "Sampler 2" (Safari default behavior). If not, double-click on the file "sampler.zip" to extract it as a folder.
2. On Windows, right-click on the file sampler.zip and choose "extract all" to uncompress it as a folder
3. Open the uncompressed folder. You should see two sub-folders:
    - samples
    - JSON
4. Check to make sure that the samples folder contains your audio samples and that the JSON folder contains a file called "samples.json"
 - On a Mac, you can select the file "samples.json" and hit the space bar to get a quick view preview of its contents.
 - On Windows, you can open "samples.json" with any text editor to see its contents.

## Step 4 - Share your work 

1. **Open the editor.p5js.org link:**
https://editor.p5js.org/dbwetzel/sketches/gTlEyB2ei 
 - This will lead you to the project source code. Don't worry!
2. **Log in** to the P5 editor if you have not done so already. You should have an account that you created for Web App #1 (Simple Player)
 - Look for the "Login or Sign up" links in the top right corner
 - If you are logged in properly, it will say "Hello, [your username]"
3. <image src="images/P5_File_Duplicate.png" align="right" width=150 alt="File -> Duplicate"> **IMPORTANT:** Make your own copy of the template: File->Duplicate. 
4. **Run** the sketch (big "Play" triangle button in the top left). Verify that you see the "One Shot Sampler" page in the "preview" pane on the right.
5. **Upload** your files to the Web App "Sketch Files"
 - <image src="images/upload_samples.png" align="right" width=150 alt="File -> Duplicate"> In the "Sketch Files" area (">" button in the top left), you will see an empty folder called "samples."  Upload your audio sample files to this folder.
 - find the drop-down menu arrow on the right side of the folder name
choose "upload file" to add a sample file from your computer to this folder in the web app. 
 - Also in the sketch files area, you will find a folder called "JSON." There is a file there already called "samples.json" that contains an empty set of square brackets. Replace that file with the one you created in Step 3
    - Click the dropdown arrow for the existing "samples.json" file in the editor and choose "Delete" (and press OK in the dialog asking if you are sure)
    - Click the dropdown arrow for the JSON folder and choose "Upload File"
    - Navigate to the "samples.json" file from the Sampler 2 folder in your Downloads and choose it for upload
    - You should see your samples.json file now on the Web App file list in the P5 editor. Click on it to see its contents in the editor (don't make any changes manually unless you really know what you're doing!).
6. Verify your work
 - Press the "run" button on your Sampler app to reload the page. You should see all your samples load in the player window.
 - If everything worked as planned, you will see and be able to play your samples when you load the page and run the sketch.
 - All the player parameters for each sample are saved in the samples.json file. 
 - You should also be able to share a link to your work, and others can play your samples, too.
 - **Troubleshooting:**
    - Make sure your data is in a file called "samples.json" inside the folder called "JSON." If you uploaded the new file before deleting the old one, the editor might have made a new file called "samples-1.json." Delete the old "samples.json" and rename your new file "samples.json." Web apps are picky about these things.
    - Make sure your samples were uploaded to the "samples" folder. The web app is looking for audio files in that folder. The location and file names must exactly match what is given in the .json data file.
7. If you want to edit or modify your list of samples
 - Delete or add samples as desired using the web app interface
 - Repeat steps 2-4 above.

2. Submit your JSON data file "samples.json" (attachment)
3. **IMPORTANT** - in the comments box: a link to your P5js Sampler App (File->Share ... View Only)
4. Also in the comments box, below (or as another attachment in Word or PDF), list your samples with a credit/citation (author/creator, description, location(?), download link)
 - if you recorded it yourself, credit yourself as the creator and give some information about the sound source and recording location
 - If you used a public domain source, give as much info as necessary to describe the source, author (if any), date, and where you found it
 - If you used copyrighted sounds (commercial recordings, TV, film, etc) please give a full citation in APA or MLA format.
